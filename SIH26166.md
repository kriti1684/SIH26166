## Background

**Image Registration** is the process of aligning two or more images of
the same scene taken:

-   at different times,
-   from different viewpoints, or
-   by different sensors,

into a common coordinate system.

### Two Main Components

-   **Source Image (Moving):** The image that is geometrically
    transformed to align with the reference image.
-   **Reference Image (Fixed):** The target image to which the source
    image is aligned.

## Problem Description

The registration of lunar images involves finding corresponding match
points between the source and reference images, followed by accurately
aligning the source image with the reference.

### Key Challenges

#### 1. Illumination Variation

Changes in Sun azimuth and elevation alter the lighting conditions of
the lunar surface, making the same terrain appear significantly
different and reducing feature correlation.

#### 2. Viewpoint Variation

Different camera positions and orientations introduce geometric
distortions such as:

-   translation,
-   rotation,
-   scaling,
-   perspective distortion.

#### 3. Scale Variation

Images captured from different orbital altitudes and sensors possess
varying spatial resolutions, creating substantial scale differences
between datasets.

## Expected Solution

Develop a **generic software solution** capable of finding
correspondence between Chandrayaan-2 optical images and lunar reference
images with:

-   **Sub-pixel registration accuracy**
-   Uniformly distributed match points across the image
-   Robustness to illumination, viewpoint, and scale variations

### Expected Deliverables

-   Registered image product
-   Corresponding matched feature points
-   Registration software pipeline
-   Evaluation metrics, such as:
    -   RMSE
    -   Inlier match count
    -   Inlier ratio
